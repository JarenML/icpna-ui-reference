# Figma Make Prompt — ICPNA Scenario Planning Platform

Create a polished, enterprise-grade interactive web application prototype for **ICPNA** called:

# Plataforma de Planificación de Metas y Simulación de Escenarios de Matrícula

The prototype is for a technical proposal prepared by **Inversiones On Line S.A.C. (IOL)** for **Business Analytics S.A.C.**, with **ICPNA** as the final end-user.

The application is a **planning and scenario simulation platform**, NOT an AI prediction platform and NOT an automatic mathematical optimizer.

Its purpose is to consume an existing external demand forecast called **Baseline**, allow authorized users to define an institutional target called **Meta ICPNA**, apply explicit business interventions, simulate multiple scenarios, measure the gap to the target, evaluate operational feasibility, and compare alternatives.

The main conceptual flow must be:

**Baseline → Meta ICPNA → Interventions → Simulation → Scenario Result → Gap to Target → Operational Feasibility → Scenario Comparison**

The Baseline is read-only and must never appear editable.

---

## 0. Three Reporting Components (read this before building any screen)

The platform's reporting must be organized around **three distinct reporting components**. Every chart, table, and screen below belongs to one of them. Keep this classification visible in how screens are grouped in the navigation.

1. **Reporte Global** — shows the overall curves, the general effect of the scenario versus Baseline and Meta, and the relative weight of the variables/interventions with the greatest impact on the result. This is the consolidated, executive-level view. Covered by **Screen 1 (Resumen Ejecutivo)** and **Screen 3 (Resultado de Simulación)**.

2. **Reporte Granular** — lets the user go beyond "which parameter had the most weight" and drill into how that effect plays out across business dimensions: **Sede, Tipo de Alumno, Modalidad**, and other dimensions already defined elsewhere in this prompt (Programa, Curso, Horario, Periodo). This is a new dedicated screen: **Screen 5 (Reporte Granular)**.

3. **Reporte de Alertas** — consolidates and visualizes the alerts generated by the platform (feasibility, capacity, data-quality, low-evidence assumptions) so that scenarios requiring review or attention are clearly identifiable in one place. This is a new dedicated screen: **Screen 6 (Reporte de Alertas)**.

> Reference note: the layout and widget structure of Screens 1 and 5 are based on internal mockup images provided for this update (summary-level, waterfall/contribution, historical comparison, filtered comparative matrices, and capacity heatmap). Screen 6 is based on a single reference slide ("Alertas y notificaciones") describing the alert catalog. **Use these only as structural/layout references — do not copy their illustrative numbers.** All numeric values across every screen must stay consistent with the dataset fixed in the "Data Consistency" section near the end of this document.

## 1. General Visual Direction

Use a clean, professional, modern **enterprise analytics dashboard** style suitable for:

- Directors
- Management
- Commercial teams
- Academic operations
- Finance
- Planning teams

The interface should look like a real enterprise application that could be deployed within the Microsoft Azure ecosystem.

### Visual Style

Use:

- White and very light neutral backgrounds
- Dark navy / deep blue for navigation and headings
- Restrained medium-blue accents
- Subtle gray borders
- Rounded cards with small radius
- Minimal shadows
- High information density without looking cluttered
- Professional business typography
- Clear hierarchy between titles, KPIs, filters and analysis
- Restrained professional chart colors
- Red only for capacity problems or critical alerts
- Amber for warnings
- Green for feasible / validated conditions
- Blue for normal informational states

Avoid:

- Futuristic AI aesthetics
- Neon colors
- Robots
- Chatbot imagery
- Excessive gradients
- Excessive glassmorphism
- Consumer-app styling

# IMPORTANT FUNCTIONAL CONSTRAINTS

The prototype MUST respect all of the following:

- Baseline is externally generated and read-only.
- The application does not modify the Baseline.
- The application does not recalculate the Baseline.
- The application does not retrain the Baseline.
- Meta ICPNA is defined by authorized business users.
- Interventions must be explicit and traceable.
- Each intervention has:
  - Period
  - Scope
  - Classification
  - Impact
  - Persistence
  - Justification
  - Responsible user
- Interventions may originate from:
  - Validated business rules
  - Managerial assumptions
  - Simple interpretable statistical estimates
- Scenario results must distinguish between these sources.
- Multiple scenarios may be saved and versioned.
- Scenarios must be comparable side-by-side.
- Capacity must be evaluated separately from statistical plausibility.
- Clearly distinguish **Meta ICPNA** from **Meta Factible**.
- Include operational metrics such as:
  - Matrículas
  - Alumnos estudiando
  - Clases requeridas
  - Ocupabilidad
  - Brecha de capacidad
- Include estimated economic impact where appropriate.
- Basic Goal-Seeking only compares scenarios created manually by users.
- The Reporte Granular (Screen 5) only breaks down results already produced by the simulation into business dimensions (Sede, Tipo de Alumno, Modalidad, Curso, Horario, Periodo) — it does not perform any additional calculation or inference beyond what the simulation already computed.
- The Reporte de Alertas (Screen 6) only consolidates alerts already generated elsewhere in the platform (Screens 1 and 3) — it does not introduce new alert logic beyond the catalog described on that screen.
- The Retención intervention applies only to Alumnos Regulares (alumnos únicos regulares); any granular breakdown by Tipo de Alumno must respect this rule rather than distributing its effect evenly.
- Do NOT implement an automatic optimizer.
- Do NOT display "AI recommendation".
- Do NOT suggest that Machine Learning automatically determines the best action.
